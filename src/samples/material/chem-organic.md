# Synthesis and characterisation of acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin)

**Course:** CHEM 232 — Organic Chemistry II Laboratory  
**Date performed:** 14 October 2025  
**Partner:** [redacted]

## Target compound

Acetylsalicylic acid (2-acetoxybenzoic acid, ASA), an over-the-counter analgesic and antipyretic.

## Reaction scheme

Salicylic acid (1.00 g, 7.24 mmol) was refluxed with acetic anhydride (5.0 mL, 5.4 g, ≈53 mmol, ~7.3 equiv) in the presence of catalytic concentrated sulfuric acid (3 drops, ≈0.05 mL) at 85 °C for 15 minutes. The reaction is an O-acetylation of the phenolic –OH by acetic anhydride, with H₂SO₄ as the proton-transfer catalyst. After cooling to room temperature and adding 20 mL of ice-water, the crude product precipitates and is collected by vacuum filtration, washed with cold water (2 × 5 mL), and recrystallised from ethanol/water.

## Mechanism reasoning

Protonation of one carbonyl oxygen of acetic anhydride by H₂SO₄ makes the adjacent carbonyl carbon more electrophilic. Salicylic acid's phenolic oxygen attacks this carbon to form a tetrahedral intermediate; collapse of that intermediate expels acetate as the leaving group. The carboxylate of the salicylic acid portion is protonated by the released acetic acid, giving the neutral product. The ortho-carboxylic acid does not get acetylated in any meaningful amount under these conditions because its –OH is much less nucleophilic than the phenoxide — phenoxide pKa ≈ 10 vs carboxylic acid pKa ≈ 3, but the phenoxide is more nucleophilic because the oxygen is not delocalised into a carbonyl.

## Yield and appearance

Mass of dried recrystallised product: 0.78 g. Theoretical yield (1:1 stoichiometry): 7.24 mmol × 180.16 g/mol = 1.304 g. **Percent yield: 59.8%.** Appearance: white crystalline solid, mp 134–136 °C (literature 135 °C). The mixed-melting-point test with authentic ASA showed no depression, supporting identity.

## Purification

Recrystallisation from a minimum volume of hot ethanol followed by slow addition of warm water until the first permanent turbidity, then cooling to room temperature and then on ice. About 30 mL of ethanol/water mixture was needed per gram. The filtrate was checked by TLC against authentic ASA (EtOAc/hexanes/AcOH 80:20:1, Rf ≈ 0.55); only one spot was visible, but I cannot rule out trace impurities below the limit of detection.

## Characterisation

- **¹H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl₃):** δ 8.10 (dd, 1H, Ar–H ortho to COOH, J = 7.9, 1.6 Hz), 7.61 (td, 1H, J = 7.9, 1.6 Hz), 7.32 (t, 1H, J = 7.6 Hz), 7.11 (dd, 1H, J = 8.0, 1.0 Hz), 2.34 (s, 3H, COCH₃). The carboxylic acid proton is exchange-broadened and was not reliably integrated.
- **IR (ATR, neat):** broad O–H stretch 2500–3300 cm⁻¹ (acid), C=O stretch 1754 cm⁻¹ (ester) and 1692 cm⁻¹ (acid), aromatic C=C 1606 and 1485 cm⁻¹, C–O 1187 cm⁻¹.
- **Melting point:** 134–136 °C, sharp.

## Notes / sources of loss

The biggest source of yield loss is probably transfer during the recrystallisation — the crystals are fine and tend to clump on the filter. The phenol starting material would also show up in TLC if it were the major impurity (Rf ≈ 0.35 in the same system) and I did not see it, so I am reasonably confident the product is clean.
