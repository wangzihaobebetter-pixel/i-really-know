# Direction Decision — Heartfelt v5

## Winner

**Living Margin**, strengthened with selected tactile behaviors from **Pocket Rehearsal**.

This decision follows actual 390×844 rendered screenshots of Today, Run and Result for all three variants.

## Head-to-head

| Dimension | Living Margin | Pocket Rehearsal | Quiet Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional safety | Strongest; warm without babying | Warm but can feel game-like | Too severe and tool-like |
| Product differentiation | Strongest; causal margin line is ownable | Card stack is familiar | Strong graphic identity, weak category fit |
| Youth appeal | Contemporary and vivid | Highest immediate energy | Fashion-forward but colder |
| Seriousness | Credible | At risk of toy/quiz language | Very credible, almost institutional |
| Flow clarity | Best source→question connection | Clear, but separated into cards | Clear hierarchy, weaker conversational continuity |
| Accumulated value | User's words feel treasured | User's words feel collectible | User's words feel documented |
| Risk | Oversized question/result type | Gamification and metric creep | Emotional regression to coldness |

## What ships from Living Margin

- Sunlit mineral background; light default
- Blue occasion/room card as Today's strong contained object
- A continuous margin line linking passage to question and settling into a mark
- Answer dock in the thumb zone
- User's own held sentence as the dominant Result object
- Rounded geometry that changes by purpose rather than universal cards
- Short, context-aware copy built around the real room/date

## What is borrowed from Pocket Rehearsal

- Visible press depth on primary controls
- More confident navy/blue color fields
- Compact physical progress marks
- The idea of a private rehearsal, without renaming the product's canonical “过一遍” vocabulary
- Slightly denser useful content on Today so calm does not look unfinished

## What is rejected

- Card-stack decoration on every screen
- Thick black outlines everywhere
- Result metric strip (it turns outcomes back into a scorecard)
- Neon acid palette and neo-brutalist document framing
- Giant Chinese questions that fill most of a viewport
- A dark default
- Generic gradient CTAs, accent rails used as decoration, feature-tile grids and icon toppers

## Corrections before production implementation

1. Cap Chinese question at 27px and Result takeaway at 29px at 390px; English may be 1–2px larger.
2. The living margin must explain causality, not become a decorative left rail. It only appears when a passage is causally linked to a question.
3. Result opens with the useful shape of understanding, not outcome counts.
4. Policy/disclaimer copy moves behind honesty details; it never outranks the user's words.
5. Today has four genuinely distinct data states: first use, active run, due return, quiet return. Each gets a purposeful lead object.
6. Bottom nav is hidden for Read, Run, self-read, reply and Result ending.
7. Tactile depth is reserved for committing actions; passive cards stay calm.
8. The user's held answer is a recurring first-class object in Result, Work and You.

## Slop diagnostic

### Living Margin prototype: 1/10

- Fired: **default type** (the prototype relies on Inter/system fallback).
- Did not fire: tech gradient, generic purple, feature grid, decorative accent rail, glass abuse, monument stat, icon toppers, center stack, wrong surface.
- Treatment: production uses a deliberate display/reading type pairing and Chinese-specific weight/line-height tuning; composition remains.

### Pocket Rehearsal prototype: 2/10

- Risks: icon-topper-like brand mark and repeated oversized rounded containers.
- More importantly, it risks category drift toward a game deck. Not selected.

### Quiet Signal prototype: 1/10 visually, but fails emotional fit

- Low generic-design score does not make it the right product. It is too hard-edged for Lin Yue's moment of fear.

## Product-design authority

The main agent owns visual judgment, screen composition, interaction posture and final screenshot approval. Subagents may inventory code, implement bounded non-visual mechanics or review; they do not independently style screens.
